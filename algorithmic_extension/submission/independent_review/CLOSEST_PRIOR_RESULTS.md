# Focused prior-art questions

Read Appendix D with Theorems 4 and 6. These are questions for an independent
reader, not a novelty certificate. Ask for exact results and explicit
mappings of hypotheses; absence of identical wording is not sufficient.

| Prior source | Challenge |
| --- | --- |
| [Diakonikolas thesis (2011)](https://academiccommons.columbia.edu/doi/10.7916/D80K2GR9), Chapter 5, especially 5.2.2, Theorems 5.3.3/5.3.5, Remark 5.3.6 | Feasible/convexified representatives and recovery limits are established. Does a stated result imply all-subinterval exactness or exceptional-price repair? |
| [Diakonikolas–Yannakakis (2008)](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~ilias/papers/convex-pareto.html) | Succinct convex Pareto sets and cardinality/oracle bounds precede us. Changing the error metric alone is not a novelty claim. |
| [Bazgan et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00224-021-10066-5), Proposition 1/Theorem 2; [Helfrich et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00186-023-00823-2), Theorems 4.4/5.5 | Weighted-sum factors and supported-solution limits are prior art. Do they also imply the price-localized repair refinement? |
| [Magnanti–Stratila](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.3148), Lemma 2 | Tangent replacement is established. Is charging only missing-slope prices substantive or already a stated corollary? |
| [Hanasusanto et al.](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.2015.1392), Theorem 1; [Buchheim–Kurtz](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-016-1053-z); [Kurtz (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-026-02329-1), Theorems 2/7 | Challenge the uncertainty-concavity and integer-recourse distinctions. Appendix D supplies a failed convex uncertainty lift; provide a valid reduction if one exists. |
| [Tantipongpipat et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.11281), Theorems 1.2/1.3; [Shen et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10543-026-01109-9), Theorem 4.1/Appendix A.3 | The endpoint machinery is established. Check the exact active-face union and single-site corollary, not a purported new eigensolver. |
| [Cardinal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16642), Section 4.2 | Greedy covering is prior art. No polyhedral-input complexity guarantee is asserted for spectral input. |
| [Barbarino–Noferini](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2023.04.022), Theorem 1.1 | Rellich is an established input. Check the finite-branch coincidence argument, including identical branches and persistent ties. |
