\opening{Dear Editors,}
Please consider ``Finite Menus of Spectral Subspaces: Realizability and the
Cost of Convexification'' as an original research article.

The paper studies the global minimax design of a fixed number of orthogonal
subspace presets over a scalar resource-price interval. Its central question
is when an approximation based only on the fully adaptive value curve can
actually be implemented by feasible presets. We give an exact criterion on
every price subinterval in terms of available slopes on optimal faces,
express that criterion through spectral cutoff eigenspaces, and bound the
additional presets needed to repair a convexified menu by the number of
exceptional prices. That number is finite for each fixed spectral instance.

The contribution is a structural refinement of established representative-set,
scalarization, and finite-adaptability ideas. We explicitly compare their
assumptions and conclusions with ours. We do not claim a new general
minimax duality, greedy covering principle, or spectral perturbation theorem.
Synthetic computational evidence supports the construction with independently
replayable continuous-price upper and lower bounds, including banded instances
of dimension 4096. All declared slowdowns, limitations, and development
failures are retained; the paper makes no validated physical-application claim.

We believe these questions fit the journal's theoretical and computational
global optimization scope. The source and evidence artifact accompanies the
manuscript. Substantive AI assistance is disclosed in the manuscript.
